Hon'ble Mrs. Sangeeta Chandra, J.

Hon'ble Brij Raj Singh, J.

1. Heard learned counsel for the petitioner, sri S.B. Pandey, learned Senior Advocate and the Deputy Solicitor general of India assisted by Sri Varun Pandey and Sri Nishant Shukla, learned counsel appearing for opposite party no.3.

2. This writ petition has been filed with the following main prayer:-

"To issue a writ, order or direction in the nature of Mandamus commanding thereby the respondent no.2 and 3 to not give the effect to the order letter dated 09.02.2024 contained as Annexure No.1 only to the extent it creates the barrier/minimum criterion of 100 beds or more than 100 beds for the empanelment of Hospital in Ayushman Bharat Pradan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojna "

3. It is the case of the petitioner that he is a resident of Lucknow and he has been given Ayushman Card issued by opposite party no.2 under Ayushman Bharat Pradan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojna and he has been insured Rs.5,00,000/- for medical facilities. The petitioner fell ill in the month fo April, 2024 and tried to get admission in a private hospital in the vicinity of his residence, but all such hospitals have informed the petitioner that they are not empanelled under the Ayushman Bharat Pradan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojna as they are not qualified in terms of the letter dated 09.02.2024. The letter dated 09.02.2024 lays down guidelines for empanelment of hospitals, which requires that the hospitals should have at least 100 beds.

4. A preliminary objection has been raised on behalf of the opposite parties and a copy of this order passed by this Court in Public Interest Litigation (PIL) No.200 of 2024, Arvind Kumar Mishra Vs. Union of India and others, decided on 03.04.2024 has been produced before this Court, wherein the same impugned letter dated 09.02.2024 was challenged and a coordinate Bench of this Court has passed a detailed order on 03.04.2024 rejecting the public interest litigation.

5. A copy of the order so produced is kept on record.

6. From a perusal thereof, it is evident that coordinate Bench of this Court has considered the guidelines as mentioned in the impugned letter and has made certain observations, which are quoted herein-below:-

"6. Having heard learned Counsel for the parties and having perused the record especially the guidelines and the impugned order, we find merit in the submission of the learned State Counsel. Firstly, the guidelines are not in the nature of statutory rules and the very opening lines of the said guidelines under the heading 'Disclaimer' make this clear. It is provided therein that - 'the purpose of these guidelines is to provide the State Health Agency (SHA) with information to assist in the Process for Disciplinary Proceedings and De-Empanelment. These guidelines do not aim to hold all the information each SHA may require. These guidelines may also not be appropriate for all SHA's, and it is not possible for National Health Authority to understand the applicability and particular needs of each State and/or User which utilizes these Guidelines. Thus, SHA's are advised to use these guidelines prudently. The SHA may use its own expertise and prudence in regard to the applicability of the same after evaluating the issue, statutory State laws applicable if any as amended from time to time and/or guidelines or order specific to such SHA. National Health Authority though will provide assistance in regard to any queries for understanding the guidelines as may be required.' Thus, the SHA has a freeplay in the joints while determining empanelling of hospitals. SHA is entitled to determine the conditions of empanelment of hospital which it has done by the impugned order which cannot be said to be against the guidelines.

7. Secondly, the criteria referred at page 58 of the writ petition which is part of the guidelines refers to minimum criteria and not the maximum criteria. Such criteria is also open for being laid down by SHA.

8. Thirdly, we also find merit in the submission of learned State Counsel that the impugned order is not applicable to each and every district, but it is applicable only to certain districts as referred hereinabove wherein sufficient number of hospitals have already been empaneled and for those hospitals this impugned order does not apply. It is only for future empanelment of additional hospitals in those districts as referred above that this criteria has been laid down which cannot be said to be irrational. We are of the opinion that the impugned order cannot be said to be against public interest.

9. However, if the petitioner has any further grievance which may be relatable to public cause, he can always approach the NHA or the SHA for that matter, which can be looked into, but we see no reason to quash the impugned order.

10. For the reasons aforesaid, we do not find any merit in this writ petition. The writ petition is, accordingly, dismissed."

7. It appears that this petition has been filed as a proxy petition on behalf of various nursing homes being run in the city of Lucknow, whose names have been mentioned in paragraph 6 of the writ petition.

8. In case the private hospitals are aggrieved by their non-empanelment in the Ayushman Bharat Pradan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojna, they can approach on their own before the competent authority. This Court does not deem it appropriate to enter into the logic of the policy adopted by the opposite parties for empanelment of private hospitals.

9. Writ petition stands disposed of.

.
